\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Several adjacent literatures already do parts of this job, and do them well. The review below is selective rather than systematic, and it is organized around the gap this paper tries to close.

\emph{Resource estimation.} Beverland et al.\ formalize the mapping from logical algorithm to physical requirements under explicit hardware and quantum error correction (QEC) models \citep{beverland2022}, van Dam et al.\ apply end-to-end estimation to utility-scale chemistry and materials workloads \citep{vandam2024}, and Quetschlich et al.\ show how an estimation step can complement simulation and execution so that real-world instances and design-space trade-offs can be explored before hardware exists \citep{quetschlich2024resource}. Those works supply the calculational machinery this paper's Phase~2 depends on; the present contribution is the surrounding contract---what to demand of a problem \emph{before} the estimator runs, and what forces a stop afterward.

\emph{Utility and readiness assessment.} Herrmann et al.\ define \emph{quantum utility} as practical advantage under matched size, weight, and cost, and introduce application readiness levels (ARLs) as a maturity ladder \citep{herrmann2023}. Marthaler et al.\ propose ITBQ---Identify, Transform, Benchmark, Show Quantum Advantage---a four-step use-case assessment emphasizing rigorous classical benchmarking and the difficulty of translating industrial tasks onto hardware \citep{marthaler2025}. Yue and Zhang's Q-READY frames feasibility assessment for hybrid quantum--classical applications as model-based systems engineering: requirements modeling, problem formulation, workflow design, and hardware-aware feasibility evaluation before costly implementation \citep{yue2026qready}. These frameworks share this paper's problem-first orientation, resource awareness, and insistence on strong classical baselines; none of those ingredients is claimed as new here. Skeptical analyses of fine print \citep{aaronson2015}, surveys of algorithmic applications \citep{montanaro2016}, chemistry-focused assessments \citep{bauer2023prx}, and community roadmapping \citep{nqiworkshop2025,dimeglio2024} likewise evaluate where advantage might survive.

\emph{Benchmarking and verification.} Suites such as MQT Bench standardize benchmark-circuit provision across abstraction levels (they supply comparable circuits rather than crossover verdicts) \citep{quetschlich2023}, dequantization results discipline input-access assumptions \citep{tang2019,tang2021}, and predeclared evidence standards constrain what counts as verification \citep{eisert2020}.

What this paper adds is not any single ingredient but their integration into one fail-closed procedure: stop conditions that can reject the quantum framing outright; a scientific-output contract; complete recurring-cost accounting; a prospectively hash-sealed, machine-readable evidence contract; fixed allowed variations, baselines, uncertainty sources, typed decision semantics, and aggregation rules; and an executable negative demonstration with preserved artifacts. This paper does not prove superiority over the neighboring frameworks, and it reports no evidence of agreement among independent evaluators applying the procedure (\cref{sec:limitations}).
